What is phishing?

Phishing is a social-engineering technique in which an attacker tries to trick someone into revealing information, opening a harmful file, or taking an unsafe action. It can arrive by email, text message, phone call, social media, or collaboration apps.

Six warning signs

⏰

Urgency or threats

“Act now” or “your account closes today” pressures you to skip checks.

🔗

Unexpected links

Check the actual destination carefully. When unsure, navigate to the official site yourself.

👤

Unusual sender

Look for misspellings, unexpected domains, or a display name that does not match the address.

🔐

Requests for secrets

Be cautious if a message asks for passwords, one-time codes, or payment details.

📎

Unexpected attachments

Confirm with the sender through a separate trusted channel before opening them.

📝

Odd wording or context

Unexpected requests, strange grammar, or a mismatch with normal procedures deserve a second look.

FICTIONAL TRAINING EXAMPLE

Can you spot the red flags?

Subject: URGENT: Student account will be deleted!

“Hello student, confirm your password immediately or lose access. Click the link below.”

This is a fictional example. It includes no working link and does not represent a real institution.

  • Threat of account deletion
  • Pressure to act immediately
  • Request for a password
  • Generic greeting and no verified context

What should you do?

  1. Pause. Do not click, reply, or open unexpected attachments.
  2. Verify. Contact the person or organization using a trusted, separate channel.
  3. Report. Use your institution's approved reporting method or contact its IT team.
  4. Recover. If you already shared a password, change it from the official site and contact IT immediately. Enable multifactor authentication where available.
